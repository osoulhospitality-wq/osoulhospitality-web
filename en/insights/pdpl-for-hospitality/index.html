Data Protection in Hospitality: What Do We Collect and Why?

Map where booking, identity, payment and preference data goes, and record why each team needs access.

By Y. A. — Osoul HospitalityUpdated 27 November 20261 min read

Worked example

Housekeeping needs the room number and readiness status. Review the system that shares the list and limit what each role sees to the information needed for the job.

What to check

  • Data classes and collection points
  • Purpose, basis and retention
  • Recipients inside and outside the Kingdom
  • Permissions and access logs

Steps to take

  1. Map the end-to-end data flow
  2. Minimise fields to what is necessary
  3. Document notices and consent where required
  4. Review processor and transfer terms
  5. Test incident and rights-request response

What to measure

  • Systems covered by the record
  • Excess access removed
  • Request-response time

An evidence check for the owner

Review maintenance photos before sharing them with a contractor. Frame the asset and fault, exclude unnecessary guest details and limit access to the assignment.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Keeping passport copies without retention rules
  • Shared user accounts
  • Transferring data without suitable assessment

Sources and references

  1. PDPL Executive Regulations — SDAIA
  2. Personal Data Transfer Outside the Kingdom Regulation

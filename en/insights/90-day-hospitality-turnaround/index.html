A 90-Day Turnaround Plan for an Underperforming Hospitality Asset

Protect cash, stabilise service and measure the changes that make a turnaround sustainable.

By Y. A. — Osool Hospitality8 August 20261 min read

Worked example

When a cash squeeze coincides with recurring guest-facing faults, list the critical payments and repeated failures first. Give each turnaround action a named owner, deadline and measurable result.

What to check

  • Cash and immediate risks
  • Recurring service failures
  • Top three profit leaks
  • Team and leadership capacity

Steps to take

  1. Days 1–15: establish truth and controls
  2. Days 16–30: close risks and quick wins
  3. Days 31–60: redesign processes
  4. Days 61–90: lock ownership and cadence
  5. Use weekly decision gates

What to measure

  • Cash preserved
  • Failures closed
  • Guest-satisfaction trend

Mistakes to avoid

  • An oversized initiative list
  • Restructuring before diagnosis
  • Declaring victory before a metric holds

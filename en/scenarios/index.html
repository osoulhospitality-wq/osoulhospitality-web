Examples from facilities operations

An illustrative hospitality scenario with a lobby cooling fault, entrance cleaning and a spare part awaiting approval.

Before the shift

The facility manager prioritizes the cooling inspection, schedules entrance cleaning outside peak hours and assigns each task.

At the end of the shift

The technician records the post-repair temperature. A supervisor checks the entrance and requests rework where needed.

Follow-up

The cleaning supervisor verifies the rework before closure. Procurement follows up on the spare-part approval and delivery date.

Illustrative case 01Standby pump unavailable

Maintaining water service during pump repair

Early sign

A transfer test shows the standby pump is unavailable. Water service now depends on the duty pump.

What we check

  • Result of the last approved transfer test
  • Time since loss of standby capacity
  • Areas supplied by the system
  • Repair progress against the agreed service window

What we do

  • Invoke the continuity procedure agreed with the operator
  • Assign repair to the specialist
  • Assign responsibilities and set an escalation route for delays
  • Repeat the transfer test and document the outcome

Decision for the owner

Has standby capacity been restored, or should the temporary arrangement remain?

What the owner receivesA risk record, repair order, test result and decision on returning to normal operation
Illustrative case 02Recurring faults

Faults return in the same units after requests are closed

Early sign

More requests are being reopened or repeated in the same units. Response time and repair quality vary.

What we check

  • Repeat and reopened requests
  • Time to restore service
  • Cost per request by cause
  • Impact on guest satisfaction and revenue

What we do

  • Investigate the root cause
  • Use consistent request categories
  • Review maintenance procedures and agreed service levels
  • Set a 30-, 60- and 90-day stability plan

Decision for the owner

Where should improvement start, and what should be stopped or changed?

What the owner receivesA performance baseline, causes of faults, an improvement plan and a tracking dashboard
Illustrative case 03Oversight across several properties

Performance is difficult to compare across properties

Early sign

The properties use different definitions for the same measures, making it hard to compare performance and identify where the owner needs to act.

What we check

  • Consistency of performance calculations
  • Financial and operating variances
  • Open risks by property
  • Overdue decisions and the work that depends on them

What we do

  • Agree one definition for each measure
  • Create a single owner report covering all properties
  • Assign approval responsibilities and set a route for delays
  • Set a regular performance review schedule

Decision for the owner

Which property needs attention, and which variances require a decision across the portfolio?

What the owner receivesAn owner dashboard, a decision log, assigned responsibilities and a review schedule
Illustrative case 04Owner and operator responsibilities

Maintenance and change decisions lack a clear approver

Early sign

Decisions are pending, performance measures conflict or change requests lack an agreed view of financial and operating impact.

What we check

  • Time waiting for a decision
  • Proportion of commitments with no named owner
  • Variance against budget
  • Quality of evidence in the operator’s reports

What we do

  • Review contracts and reports
  • Assign responsibilities and record work that depends on each approval
  • Set up a decision log and escalation route
  • Prepare an options memo for the owner

Decision for the owner

Who can make each decision, and which obligations need evidence or renegotiation?

What the owner receivesAssigned responsibilities, an owner report, a decision log and an escalation route
Illustrative case 05Systems and equipment handover

Equipment is ready for handover before maintenance records are complete

Early sign

Test records, training, spares or warranties are missing, and the team’s training is left to the last week.

What we check

  • Completeness of operating and maintenance manuals and warranties
  • Successful integration tests
  • Staff training coverage
  • Closure of critical defects

What we do

  • List required handover documents and items
  • Review test evidence
  • Plan training and knowledge transfer
  • Document acceptance conditions

Decision for the owner

What can be accepted now, and what needs to remain conditional or be rejected?

What the owner receivesA handover register, test records, a corrective action plan and an acceptance memo
Illustrative case 06Refurbishment of an operating property

A proposed refurbishment needs clear priorities

Early sign

The owner needs to prioritise work by its impact on guests, continuity of operations and equipment life-cycle cost.

What we check

  • Effect on the guest offering and revenue
  • Operational downtime
  • Life-cycle cost
  • Regulatory and technical risks

What we do

  • Confirm the owner’s requirements
  • Review operations and guest experience
  • Rank capital spending by impact
  • Plan the work in phases

Decision for the owner

Which investment comes first, and which work can wait without reducing value?

What the owner receivesCapital spending priorities, the owner’s requirements, a phased plan and an investment memo

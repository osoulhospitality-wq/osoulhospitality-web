On takeover day, the incoming team receives a large spreadsheet full of equipment names and numbers. During the next shift water service fails. The duty employee looks for a pump listed under a different name in the contractor’s agreement and cannot find the authorised contact. A polished spreadsheet would not solve this. The property needs a dependable connection between the physical equipment, its record and the person allowed to act on it.
# Start with the service people depend on
Ask the operator which interruptions would prevent normal use of the building. Walk back from each critical service to the equipment supporting it with a competent specialist. Begin with the most consequential assets and make unknowns visible. Some equipment serves one apartment, some serves several floors and some supports a shared amenity. Record the area of influence actually established rather than inferring it from a contractor's nickname.
Before surveying, agree what counts as a manageable asset. Will the equipment and its attachments share a record? Does a component need its own service history or warranty? Thousands of rows deliver little value if nobody can maintain them. Choose a level of detail that allows the team to assign a job, trace expenditure and make a reasoned decision about repair or remaining service life.
# Build a card someone else can verify
Give each asset a stable identifier, a recognisable name, a usable location and the service it supports. Add manufacturer, model and serial information when it can be verified. Identify the source when information comes from a document. Provide an explicit status for an unreadable plate or equipment that could not be inspected until a specialist opened a restricted area. Do not complete uncertain fields by guessing.
Attach the maintenance provider, relevant scope, available warranty and the approved source of operating or service instructions. If a scannable label is used, test it with an authorised duty account. It should lead to the correct record and display information appropriate to the role. A label accessible from a public corridor should never be an unrestricted route to internal records or information about residents.
# Accept the property with recorded exceptions
Equipment may be running even though its most recent visit history cannot be found. A current agreement may include a machine removed months ago. Record the discrepancy and assign the person who will check the equipment or locate the record. Missing documentary evidence is not proof that work was never performed. A maintenance decision must also consider condition, specialist advice and relevant manufacturer guidance.
A handover exception identifies the location, possible service impact, limits of inspection and the next access opportunity. It records what the new operator accepts now and what remains with another party. If an occupied area cannot be accessed, agree a later appointment respecting resident privacy. This makes inherited work visible and prevents a delayed inspection from being interpreted as acceptance of a condition nobody observed.
# Connect asset history to purchasing
Can a technician find the last repair before ordering another part? Can accounts distinguish a stock purchase from a part actually fitted to a specific machine? The link allows an owner to compare repair and replacement and understand recurring expenses. It does not by itself establish savings. Only a properly defined comparison of actual expenditure and service conditions can support a claim about financial results.
Plan for retirement as well as addition. When a machine is replaced, retain its history and start a separate record for the new equipment. Link the new warranty, handover and commissioning date. If room numbers are treated as permanent machine identities, the costs of two generations of equipment become tangled. A newly installed unit can appear expensive because a former unit in the same space consumed several repairs.
# Illustrative field example
Imagine receiving one pump name in a handover schedule and a different trade name in the supplier agreement. A specialist and operator representative compare the plate, serial number and service function. When the match is confirmed the supervisor records both historic search names against one asset. If evidence remains uncertain, the suspected match is recorded without merging. Premature consolidation could attach an unrelated failure history or warranty.
Then ask a duty employee to open a practice instruction from the record and a colleague who missed the survey to find the equipment using the description. If either must call the surveyor for directions, improve the location description. Reception and housekeeping may use a well-established local name absent from the drawing. Adding that search term improves reporting while preserving the approved technical identity for purchasing.
# Handover acceptance checklist
• Sample assets with different service impacts and reconcile records to physical location, agreement and available warranty.
• Give every significant gap a status, completion owner and review date in the exception schedule.
• Verify that location descriptions support authorised access and contain no resident details.
• Check how additions, moves and retirement preserve previous instructions and asset histories.
• Test creating a job, attaching a visit record and retrieving it using an appropriate shift account.
• Assign responsibility for data quality when personnel, contractors or operating agreements change.
Give the owner an exportable copy as agreed in the contract, plus field definitions and editing responsibilities. A useful register preserves knowledge of the property through a change of software or provider and lets a different employee make a good decision during an inconvenient shift.
